## Understanding the Detection System in HackHub

The detection system in HackHub runs on three overlapping meters: visual sight cones, audio propagation, and a suspicion tracker that escalates from idle to combat alert. Every enemy has a 90-degree forward-facing sight cone with a default range of about 15 meters, although snipers and heavy operatives extend that reach to 25 meters or more. If your character enters that cone while standing or sprinting, the meter fills almost instantly; while crouching, the fill rate drops to roughly one-third because your hitbox is lower and your silhouette blends with cover objects.

### Detection Meter Mechanics

The **suspicion meter** is what every HackHub player needs to watch, because it determines whether guards stay in patrol mode or flip into kill-on-sight behavior. According to community testing, the meter has four visible stages, each with its own threshold and enemy response.

| Stage | Meter Fill | Enemy Behavior | Recovery Time |
|-------|-----------|----------------|---------------|
| Idle | 0–20% | Normal patrol routes | Instant when out of sight |
| Wary | 21–55% | Slows down, investigates noise source | ~8 seconds hiding |
| Alerted | 56–85% | Calls backup, sweeps area in widening pattern | ~15 seconds out of sight |
| Combat | 86–100% | Full aggro, locks exits, raises alarm | Mission failure in most contracts |

Once the meter crosses 86%, most contracts trigger an automatic fail unless you're playing a mode with respawns. That hard line is why so many players obsess over staying below the 55% mark whenever possible.

### Sound Propagation System

Sound in HackHub is calculated per surface type and per movement action, and the radius it travels is far more important than people realize. Walking on metal grating at full speed can broadcast your position up to 20 meters, while crouch-walking on carpet barely registers at 4 meters. The game's audio engine also factors in elevation, so a footstep on an upper floor is muffled for guards on the level below, but a thrown object still carries through vents and open shafts.

Community data suggests the average guard has a hearing radius of about 12 meters during patrol, dropping to 6 meters when distracted by a terminal or a corpse they haven't yet discovered. Throwable distract tools like noise emitters and decoy grenades can push that radius out further, which is why experienced players stack multiple distractions when extracting through a hot zone.

### Light and Shadow Usage

Lighting in HackHub is dynamic, and guards actually check shadow density before they check distance. Rooms lit by overhead fluorescents are the worst places to cross, while storage closets and under-desk pockets give a 40% reduction to detection fill rate simply because of contrast. Smoke grenades and EMP blasts can disable a section of lights, which temporarily darkens entire wings and pushes guard sight range down by roughly 30%.

Players who run a "lights-out" playstyle often pair this with a hacking tool that overloads junction boxes, creating rolling blackouts that distract guards toward flickering terminals. The timing window during those blackouts is short, but it's long enough to slip past two or three patrols without triggering the meter at all.

## Advanced Stealth Tactics and Movement Patterns

Once the basics feel comfortable, the next step is reading patrol patterns and timing windows that experienced players exploit on the hardest contracts. These tactics go beyond crouch-walking and lean on game knowledge that takes dozens of hours to accumulate.

### Patrol Pattern Reading

Every guard follows one of four pattern archetypes, and learning to recognize them on sight is what unlocks Ghost-ranked runs. Each archetype has a tell that gives away its next move, and most players only need to watch a guard for 30 seconds to predict the next 2 minutes of their route.

| Pattern | Archetype Tell | Reset Behavior |
|---------|---------------|----------------|
| Loop | Pauses at every corner | Returns to start after full circuit |
| Sweep | Stops to inspect objects | Reverses direction at midpoint |
| Stationary | Stands near terminals | Rotates 180 degrees every 10s |
| Roamer | Walks without fixed path | Telegraphs next area with idle animation |

The **Stationary** archetype is often the easiest to slip past, but the **Roamer** archetype causes the most mission failures because it doesn't follow a predictable path. When you spot a Roamer, the safe play is to wait until it walks into a side room, then close the door behind it before crossing the main corridor. Community guides on the [official HackHub site](https://www.hackhub.com) confirm that doors auto-close only when the guard is more than 4 meters away, so timing matters.

### Vent, Rope, and Vertical Routing

Vertical movement is one of the most underused tools in HackHub, because most beginners stay on the ground floor and try to brute-force past patrols. Vents, climb ropes, and ziplines let you bypass entire patrol zones without ever entering a sight cone, which is a much safer way to HackHub avoid detection on dense maps.

- **Vents** carry noise across longer distances but break line of sight completely
- **Climb ropes** are silent but expose your silhouette against bright walls
- **Ziplines** are fast but emit a metallic hum that guards within 8 meters can hear
- **Drop downs** from ledges are silent if you hold crouch before landing

Combining a vent entry with a zipline exit is the fastest clean-route combo in the game, and it's how speedrunners cut two minutes off mission times without raising a single alert. The trick is to enter the vent during a patrol gap and exit onto a zipline that drops you straight into an extraction zone, skipping the entire ground-floor gauntlet.

## Common Mistakes That Trigger Enemy Alerts

Even experienced players fall into a handful of habits that consistently push the suspicion meter past the safe threshold. Most of these mistakes are subtle, which is why they survive into late-game runs and end otherwise-clean infiltrations.

### High-Frequency Failure Points

After reviewing community reports from the latest ranked season, five mistakes show up over and over in failed contracts. The table below maps each mistake to the detection stage it usually triggers and the easiest fix.

| Mistake | Triggered Stage | Easy Fix |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| Reloading near patrols | Wary → Alerted | Reload only in safe rooms |
| Stacking bodies in hallways | Idle → Wary | Hide bodies in lockers or vents |
| Looking at guards through glass | Idle → Wary | Use one-way mirrors or dark rooms |
| Using loud weapons as a distraction | Alerted → Combat | Swap to suppressed sidearm |
| Crossing lit zones at sprint | Wary → Combat | Walk through lit zones only |

The single most common mistake is **reloading near patrols**, because the reload animation briefly reduces your mobility and adds a metallic click to the audio log. Even suppressed weapons make some noise when the magazine ejects, so the habit should be to top off ammo only when you're inside a confirmed safe room, ideally one with a closed door and no patrol within 15 meters.

### How Body Placement Affects Detection

A second, less obvious failure point is how you handle neutralized guards. Even if you don't trigger the combat stage, leaving a body in a patrol lane forces the next passing guard to enter the wany stage immediately when they spot it. Stashing bodies in lockers, dumpsters, or high vents keeps patrols calm and prevents cascading alerts that can spread across the entire map within a minute.

Community testing indicates that a single unhidden body adds roughly 25% to the suspicion meter of the next guard who sees it, and that guard will then radio a status check to nearby units. That radio call is what flips the local alert to a sector-wide one, so the value of hiding bodies compounds the deeper you get into a contract.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the fastest way to HackHub avoid detection early in a contract?**

Start every contract with a 30-second scout phase: stand at the entry point, tag enemy patrol routes with your visor, and identify the two safest extraction paths. Rushing into the map without this scout phase is the leading cause of early combat alerts, especially on dense indoor maps.

**Does crouching actually lower the detection meter in HackHub?**

Yes, crouching reduces the meter fill rate by about 65% against standard guards and 50% against heavy operatives, because your silhouette is smaller and your movement noise radius drops to under 4 meters. Pair crouch-walking with the Phantom Mesh Mk.II suit for the deepest stealth profile in the current patch.

**Can guards see you through smoke grenades in HackHub?**

Smoke grenades drop visual detection by roughly 60%, but guards inside the smoke cloud still receive partial audio cues from your footsteps and breathing. Use smoke as a line-of-sight breaker rather than a full invisibility tool, and combine it with a noise decoy for the cleanest escape.

**Is it ever worth going loud to HackHub avoid detection?**

Going loud is almost never worth it on stealth-rated contracts, because a single combat alert locks exits and fails the mission in most modes. The only legitimate loud strategy is using suppressed sidearms to silently eliminate an isolated guard, which keeps the alert stage at wany instead of combat.

**How do I reset the suspicion meter mid-mission in HackHub?**

The fastest reset is to break line of sight, crouch behind solid cover, and stay still for at least 8 seconds. Guards in the wany stage will return to patrol after that window, and the meter will drop back to the idle range. Hiding bodies and disabling nearby terminals speeds the recovery by an extra 2 to 3 seconds.
