Stealth missions in HackHub reward patience over raw speed. This HackHub stealth mission guide breaks down the detection systems, loadout picks, and patrol patterns that separate a clean S-rank run from a chaotic reload, giving you a practical playbook before you ever queue into your first contract.

## Stealth Mission Overview: What Defines a Clean Hack

HackHub's stealth contracts sit at the heart of its heist loop, and although every mission feels different on the surface, they all share the same scoring spine: time held below detection, terminals accessed without alarm, and civilians left unaware. Players who grind the **Bronze** and **Silver** ranks quickly discover that the objective screen is only half the story, because the **Detection Meter** quietly decides your payout multiplier while you are busy hunting cameras.

The trick, according to community data circulating on the official Discord, is to treat every mission like a puzzle rather than a combat encounter. A clean run usually finishes with the meter pinned below **30%** the entire time, because any spike above that value triggers heavier guard rotations on the next contract. If you want consistent rewards, you learn to read sightlines before you fire any tool — and that habit starts with understanding the three core mechanics in the next section.

- **Contract tiers**: Bronze (tutorial), Silver (mixed patrols), Gold (multi-floor layouts), Platinum (adaptive AI)
- **Scoring axes**: time, detections, alarms raised, civilians pacified, loot extracted
- **Failing states**: full alarm, hostage down, or operator KIA — any one resets your score multiplier

## Core Stealth Mechanics You Must Master

The detection system in HackHub layers three independent meters: **Vision**, **Noise**, and **Heat**. Each one ticks independently, which is why a "silent" takedown can still tank your score if the **Noise Footprint** of your landing gear stays bright on a guard's radar for too long. Understanding how the meters overlap is the single biggest leap forward you can make as a new infiltration player, because the game never tells you which meter spiked during a failed run.

### Vision Cones and Detection Meters

Vision is the most familiar mechanic, but HackHub adds a wrinkle that casual players often miss. Every guard carries a primary cone and a secondary **peripheral arc** that extends roughly 45 degrees beyond the centerline. If you only watch the arrow on the minimap, you will get shot while standing right behind the cone's tip, because the peripheral arc still pings at roughly 60% detection strength. Veteran operators recommend crouching whenever you cross a corridor, even if the main cone points away, because crouch-walking suppresses your **Visibility Multiplier** by about half compared to standing.

| Detection Source | Base Trigger Speed | Crouch Suppression | Crouch-Walk Suppression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guard primary cone (frontal) | Fast (~1.2s to full) | -40% | -65% |
| Guard peripheral arc | Medium (~2.0s to full) | -25% | -50% |
| Camera sweep | Slow (~3.0s to full) | -30% | -55% |
| Tripwire laser | Instant | No effect | No effect |
| Motion sensor (wall) | Very slow (~4.0s) | -50% | -70% |

*Values are community-reported averages and may vary slightly between patches.*

### Noise Footprints and Distractions

Noise is the silent killer of new players in HackHub. Sprinting across a tile floor registers a **Noise Footprint** that lingers for about 6 seconds, and any guard crossing that tile within the window gets a free +15% meter contribution even though they never saw you. Crouch-walking eliminates the footprint entirely, but it costs you time, which is why most experienced runners bank their movement — sprint, freeze, sprint — to clear the loud zones in a single burst rather than dragging noise across long corridors.

The **distraction tool** is your primary way to bend Noise toward you on purpose. Throwing a coin or a tagged phone pulls guards for roughly 4-7 seconds depending on their AI tier, and since the cooldown on these throwables is short, you should chain two distractions in a row when you need to slip a full patrol past a corridor junction.

## HackHub Stealth Mission Loadout and Tool Builds

Your loadout defines what is even possible inside a mission, so this HackHub stealth mission guide puts loadout choices ahead of route planning. A balanced build covers mobility, distraction, and one hard counter for the worst-case scenario where alarms become inevitable, because holding a panic tool for the entire run is a common mistake new infiltrators make.

### Recommended Tool Tier List

| Tool Slot | S-Tier Pick | A-Tier Pick | Avoid On Tighter Maps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary takedown | **EMP Dart** (silent, 8s stun) | Tranquilizer | Loud pistol |
| Distraction | Tagged Phone | Coin | Glass bottle |
| Vision bust | Thermal Goggles | Mirror Prism | Standard cam |
| Panic reset | **Signal Jammer** | Smoke charge | Grenade |
| Mobility | Grapple Anchor | Mag-Boots | Nothing |

The **EMP Dart** is a standout because it knocks a guard out without producing a Noise Footprint, which is why it earns S-tier for stealth-first operators. The **Signal Jammer** deserves the panic slot because it can reset your Detection Meter by roughly 25% once per mission, giving you a second chance if you slip during the objective window. If you are running a coordinated duo, swap the panic slot for a **Lure Beacon** that your partner can ping to redirect the patrol AI.

## Route Planning and Patrol Reading

Route planning is where the HackHub stealth mission guide philosophy actually shows up in your K/D, because the same corridor can be either a clean shortcut or an instant reset depending on when you walk it. Most operators recommend scouting a mission at least once on **Recon Mode** before committing to a hard run, because Recon unlocks the patrol waypoints that the live mission keeps hidden behind the in-game fog of war.

### Common Patrol Patterns and Counter Routes

| Map Style | Typical Patrol | Counter Route | Risk Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linear office | Two guards, fixed loop | Wait for overlap at break room | Cameras every 8m |
| Multi-floor lab | Vertical sweeps, staggered timing | Stairwell during 4-second gap | Tripwires on ladders |
| Courtyard villa | Three guards, irregular | Alley behind greenhouse | Drone patrol at minute 3 |
| Server farm | Mostly cameras, one roaming | Cable-floor crawl, no sprint | Floor sensors, level 3 |
| Rooftop tower | Snipers + drones | Smoke + grapple vertical | Grapples have 12s cooldown |

*Approximate timings based on community testing during the open-beta cycle and may shift after balance patches.*

The smartest counter-route is almost never the straightest one. When you peek a junction and two patrols are about to converge, you usually save more time by doubling back and looping through a side room than by trying to sprint between them, because the side room absorbs your noise and resets the patrol memory of the cameras in that zone.

## Advanced Strategies for Late-Game Infiltration

Once you have the mechanics and route planning on lock, the next step in any stealth mission guide for HackHub is to handle the edge cases, because elite difficulty throws adaptive AI that reroutes patrols when you take the same path twice. This is where small habits compound into a big score, especially when you are chasing the Platinum tier contracts that gate the cosmetic rewards behind S-ranks.

### Emergency Protocols When Things Go Loud

Sometimes detection is inevitable — a teammate fires too early, or a civilian walks into a laser tripwire. The panic slot exists exactly for this, but you should also memorize the **stagger windows** that follow each detection spike. When the meter jumps from 0% to 40%, guards pause their patrol for roughly 1.5 seconds to "investigate," and that pause is your golden second to relocate. Hold your ground, never sprint toward the exit, and let the AI give you back the time it just stole.

A practical escalation ladder players share on Discord looks like this:

- **0-30% detection**: continue plan, suppress noise
- **30-50% detection**: pause, switch to cover, wait 2 beats
- **50-70% detection**: throw distraction opposite your route, activate Signal Jammer
- **70%+ detection**: smoke charge, then extract from nearest safe room

### Co-op Stealth Synergies

Duo stealth in HackHub behaves differently from solo runs because the AI treats two operators as a single heat cluster, which means synchronizing your movements keeps the Noise Footprint tied to one tile instead of stretching a trail across the level. If your partner is drawing a patrol east, do not break line of sight from them — stay within **Role-Based Hugs** of about 8 meters and let their patrol pull your share of the heat off your cone. For broader matchups and team-based approaches, the [HackHub co-op command playbook](/en/stealth-strategy/HackHub-Coop-Command-Playbook/) covers the role assignments that complement the loadouts above.

Avoid the temptation to split up on tall vertical maps, because once you lose audio line-of-sight, the game spawns a roaming patrol that hunts the most recently silent player. Reported by players as the single most common cause of Platinum resets, this "ghost patrol" mechanic punishes split paths harder than any loud gunfight.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the fastest way to learn HackHub stealth missions as a new player?**
Start on Bronze contracts with the Recon Mode toggle enabled, because scanning patrols before committing turns every map into a guided puzzle. Spend your first ten runs learning only the **Detection Meter** — ignore score, ignore loot — and you will skip past the Silver plateau much faster than jumping straight into Gold.

**Is crouch-walking always better than sprinting in HackHub stealth?**
Not always, because crouch-walking eats mission time and your score also weighs completion speed. The community-tested rule is to sprint across open tiles in a single burst, then crouch immediately on the next tile, because a 1-second sprint blast produces one footprint instead of a long noise trail.

**Can you recover from a fully tripped alarm in a stealth mission?**
Yes, but only with a pre-equipped **Signal Jammer** in the panic slot. The jammer pulls the meter back by roughly 25% and confuses nearby guards for another 4 seconds, which is usually enough to either finish the objective or extract via the secondary exit, although your final score will be heavily penalized.

**Do detectors and cameras stack their detection contributions?**
They do, and the stacking is roughly additive rather than multiplicative, which means a 20% camera ping plus a 15% guard cone can push you over 50% almost instantly. Reported by players testing Gold contracts, neutralizing cameras with the Thermal Goggles before crossing their sweep is the single biggest defensive play available to a stealth-first build.

**Which HackHub stealth loadout is best for solo Platinum runs?**
A community-recommended pairing is the **EMP Dart** for silent takedowns, the **Tagged Phone** as your distraction, **Thermal Goggles** to bust vision, and a **Signal Jammer** in the panic slot. This build has no direct damage profile, so solo players who take a hit or two will want to lean heavily on cover-and-recover rather than counterattacking, because trading shots breaks stealth scoring entirely.
