## The Core Mission Architecture

Before diving into the HackHub all missions list, you need to understand how the game categorizes its content. Missions are not a simple linear sequence, but rather a web of **Story Contracts**, **Procedural Jobs**, and **Faction Requests**. The Story Contracts form the backbone of your progression, while Procedural Jobs and Faction Requests provide the credits and reputation needed to tackle the main objectives. This layered design means that your early-game efficiency directly impacts how quickly you can access the endgame content.

The mission board in your apartment is the central hub for accepting work. It refreshes periodically, but the critical Story Contracts only appear once you meet specific prerequisites, which often include your current **Clerk Level**, **Reputation Score** with a specific faction, and the completion of a prior chain. This gating mechanism ensures you are not overwhelmed but can also lead to confusion if you are unsure what to unlock next.

### Mission Type Breakdown

| Mission Type | Frequency | Primary Reward | Difficulty Scaling | Key Prerequisite |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Story Contracts** | Fixed, one-time | Blueprints, Unlocks | Moderate to Hard | Previous Story Contract |
| **Procedural Jobs** | Infinite, rotating | Credits, XP | Easy to Hard | Clerk Level |
| **Faction Requests** | Semi-random, repeatable | Reputation, Rare Items | Moderate | Faction Standing |
| **Heist Missions** | Fixed, one-time | High-value Loot | Very Hard | Multi-step Quest Chain |
| **Tutorial Contracts** | Fixed, one-time | Basic Tools | Very Easy | New Game Start |

The table above illustrates the fundamental differences. While Procedural Jobs are your bread-and-butter for grinding credits, the real progression is locked behind the **Story Contracts** and **Heist Missions**. According to community data, players who focus exclusively on Procedural Jobs often find themselves credit-rich but progression-poor, unable to access the advanced crafting stations required for late-game hardware.

## Early-Game Mission Chain: The Clerk's Rise

The early-game HackHub all missions list begins the moment you step into your apartment. The first **Tutorial Contract** tasks you with a simple data transfer, teaching you the basic interface. However, the real game starts with the "A New Clerk in Town" story arc. This chain introduces you to the core mechanics of surveillance, client interaction, and the risk-reward system that defines the entire experience.

Your first major decision point arrives quickly. You will be asked to choose between helping a small tech startup secure their network or assisting a corporate giant with a "gray area" data retrieval. This choice doesn't lock you out of content permanently, but it does determine your starting **Reputation** with the two major factions: **The Collective** and **OmniCorp**. Your standing with these groups dictates which of their unique Procedural Jobs you can access.

### Early-Game Mission Walkthrough

- **The First Contract**: This is a guided tutorial. You will learn to use the terminal, scan for network vulnerabilities, and execute a basic `connect` command. The reward is your first set of **Netrunning Tools**, which are essential for all future missions.
- **Data Heist 101**: This contract introduces the concept of **ICE (Intrusion Countermeasures Electronics)**. You must use a **Decrypter** program to bypass a low-level firewall. The key here is speed; the longer you stay in the system, the higher the chance of detection.
- **A Friend in Need**: A contact from the tutorial asks for help. This mission teaches you the importance of log manipulation. After retrieving the data, you must use the `wipe_logs` command to remove evidence of your intrusion. Failure to do this will increase your **Heat Level**, making future jobs more dangerous.
- **The Collective's Offer**: Upon reaching **Clerk Level 3**, a representative from The Collective will reach out with a Faction Request. Completing this mission, which involves planting a surveillance worm, grants you access to their exclusive hardware shop.

The early-game is all about building a solid foundation. You need to balance running Procedural Jobs for credits with completing these story-driven contracts to unlock new abilities. A common mistake, reported by many players, is ignoring the `wipe_logs` mechanic, which leads to a high **Heat Level** and makes even simple data retrieval jobs extremely risky due to aggressive counter-intrusion measures.

## Mid-Game Contracts: Faction Warfare and Heists

As you move into the mid-game, the HackHub all missions list expands significantly. The story branches into two distinct paths based on your faction allegiance. If you sided with The Collective, you will undertake a series of missions to undermine OmniCorp's infrastructure. Conversely, OmniCorp will have you spying on Collective cells. These missions are longer, multi-stage affairs that often require specific hardware and software upgrades.

This is also where the first **Heist Mission** becomes available: "The Silicon Vault." This is a multi-stage operation that requires you to gather intelligence, bribe an insider, and finally execute a timed hack into a physical data vault. The rewards for Heist Missions are unique pieces of hardware that cannot be obtained any other way. For example, completing "The Silicon Vault" rewards you with the **Overclocked Processor**, which permanently increases your hacking speed by 15%.

The mid-game is a significant difficulty spike. You can no longer rely on basic attacks; you must actively use defensive programs to counter ICE. The **Ghost Server** mission, for instance, is notorious for its aggressive **Black ICE** that can fry your hardware if you are not running a **Shield** program. According to community testing, the **Cloaking Field Generator** from the OmniCorp path is generally considered more powerful for solo play, while the **Network Cascade Exploit** from The Collective is superior for co-op missions.

## The Endgame: The Final Protocols

The final chapters of the HackHub all missions list are where the narrative threads converge. The "Final Protocol" mission chain is a gauntlet of back-to-back high-difficulty hacks that test every skill you have learned. This is not a single mission but a series of interconnected contracts that require you to breach a fortified AI core. The difficulty here is extreme, and without a fully upgraded rig and a deep inventory of programs, success is nearly impossible.

The penultimate mission, "System Shock," requires you to simultaneously manage three separate connections while a trace is running. This is where the **Overclocked Processor** from the mid-game heist becomes invaluable. The final mission, "Kernel Panic," is a direct confrontation with the AI. You must use a unique **Zero-Day Exploit** program, which is crafted from blueprints obtained throughout the entire story. The decisions you make during this final hack determine the game's ending.

These are infinitely generated, extremely difficult contracts that reward you with **Legendary Loot**, including cosmetic items and powerful, unique programs that can be used in a New Game Plus run. The HackHub all missions list technically never ends, as the post-game provides an endless challenge for dedicated players who have mastered the game's systems.

## Mission Rewards and Optimization Strategies

Simply completing the HackHub all missions list is not enough; optimizing your reward path is key to mastering the game. Many missions have hidden bonus objectives that are not displayed on the mission board. For example, completing a data heist without triggering a single alert, or finishing a faction mission within a strict time limit, often yields a **Perfect Run Bonus** that includes rare hardware or a significant credit boost.

Your choice of hardware and programs should be tailored to the mission type. For stealth missions, you should prioritize signal dampening and cloaking programs. For aggressive data retrieval, you need the fastest decryption and attack programs available. The **Overclocked Processor** is a universally good choice, but for specific endgame hacks, the **Parallel Processor** is superior because it allows you to run two programs simultaneously, which is essential for managing the multi-connection requirements of the "System Shock" mission.

### Key Hardware for Mission Optimization

- **Overclocked Processor**: A flat 15% speed increase to all hacking actions. This is the best generalist piece of hardware, obtained from "The Silicon Vault" heist. It is particularly effective in missions with a tight time limit.
- **Parallel Processor**: Allows you to execute two programs at once. This is a game-changer for the "System Shock" mission, where you must manage a decoy, a download, and a trace simultaneously. It is a rare drop from post-game Grid missions.
- **Cloaking Field Generator**: Reduces your network presence, making it harder for ICE to detect you. This is a reward from the OmniCorp faction path and is ideal for players who prefer a stealthy, methodical approach to hacking.
- **Network Cascade Exploit**: A program that can crash multiple low-level ICE nodes at once. Rewarded from The Collective's path, this is excellent for clearing out large networks quickly, saving time for the main objective.
- **Quantum Decrypter**: A late-game program that is essential for breaking the encryption on the final AI core. It is a guaranteed reward from "The Firewall" mission.

The choice between these tools often defines your playstyle. A player with the **Cloaking Field Generator** and **Stealth Kit Mk.III** will approach a mission completely differently than one with the **Overclocked Processor** and an aggressive attack suite. The game is designed to support both, but the hardest missions, like "Kernel Panic," will test your ability to adapt if your primary strategy fails. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the hardest mission in the HackHub all missions list?**

The "Kernel Panic" mission is widely considered the hardest, as it requires perfect execution of multi-layered hacks against an aggressive AI opponent with adaptive defenses. The difficulty is compounded by a strict time limit, making the **Parallel Processor** almost a necessity.

**How do I unlock the final Heist Mission?**

To unlock "The Silicon Vault," you must reach **Clerk Level 10** and have a neutral reputation with both major factions. This means you cannot be hostile with either The Collective or OmniCorp, which requires carefully balancing your Faction Requests in the early and mid-game.

**Can I complete all missions in a single playthrough?**

Yes, you can experience every mission on the HackHub all missions list in a single playthrough. The faction-specific story paths are not mutually exclusive in the long term. You can eventually gain enough reputation with both factions to unlock all their unique contracts, though the order you do them in will affect the mid-game difficulty.

**What is the best program for early-game missions?**

The **Decrypter v1.0** is the most critical early-game program. It is used in nearly every early contract to bypass firewalls. Upgrading it to v2.0 should be a top priority before attempting any mid-game heists, as the encryption becomes significantly more complex.

**Are post-game Grid missions worth doing?**

Absolutely. The post-game Grid missions are the only source of **Legendary Loot** and the **Parallel Processor**. They offer an infinite, scaling challenge that is perfect for testing a fully upgraded rig and provides the most credits per minute for any endgame purchases you still need to make.
